## Korea Strategy: Tech to lead in 2026; policy tailwinds intact, valuations attractive
• Raise KOSPI target to 9,200 (9x NTM PE): We remain positive on KOSPl and raise our target by 26% to 9,200, supported by continued earnings upgrades led by, but not limited to, memory. While near-term consolidation is possible following the recent rally and rates concerns, we expect further EPS upgrades into Q2 to act as a key catalyst. We raise our 2026/27/28E EPS growth to +258%/+46%/+9% (from +168%/+39%/- 15%), above consensus, reflecting robust Q1 and broadening upgrades beyond tech, with 15 of 24 subsectors seeing revisions since Mar 2026 (83% of market cap including 29% non-tech). Structural support from Al-driven memory and resilient non-tech demand should sustain a higher EPS baseinto 2027–28. Our upside/downside is10,500 (9x) / 5,500 (8x NTM PE).
• Fund flow: Foreigners have been net sellers (US\$58bn YTD), moving Korea to UW in EM, with outflows concentrated in tech but largely optical given size. In contrast, selling has been offset by retail and local institutional buying, while foreign ownership has still risen to 39.2% (+3ppt YTD). Our Evidence Lab survey suggests retail inflows could persist, and we expect foreigners to re-engage should EM outflows reverse or rotate toward lagging non-tech sectors, with current Uw positioning near historic lows.
• Valuations and risk/reward: Despite a 78% YTD rally, KOSPI trades at 8x NTM PE, with derating driven by outsized gains in SEC/SKH; ex these, valuation rises to \~12x, still reasonable given strong non-tech EPS growth. Our screen highlights Holdcos, Battery/Chemicals/Metals, Shipbuilding, Consumer/Cosmetics and Elec Equip as attractive. While Al concentration, peak memory cycle pricing, and macro risks (rates, energy, geopolitics) may cap upside, earnings support remains intact. We remain bullish on Memory (SEC, SKH, Hansol Chem; Eugene Tech) and Al enablers, while favoring Defense, Shipyards and wealth-effect beneficiaries. We remain negative on EV and select tech.

## Strong memory pricing momentum continues unabated
Agentic Al continues to boost memory demand on multiple fronts beyond HBM, including DDR5/LPDDR5 for conventional servers and CPU head nodes in Al servers, as well as NAND flash for KV cache and storage. We now forecast an acceleration of memory demand growth into 2027 with DRAM bit demand growing 36%YoY in 2027E (vs 22% in 2026E) and NAND 23% (vs 20% in 2026E). With almost all incremental DRAM wafer capacity going to HBM and no new capacity (ex-China) being added to NAND, supply is not catching up with demand. With memory industry revenues edging towards US\$1.0trn by 2026E and 1.8trn by 2027E, the main risk to this upcycle remains affordability – particularly for hyperscalers, who will need to continue to tap capital markets to finance capex.
LTA negotiations continue, which we believe have a potion of volumes fixed at a fixed level of pricing. We believe Samsung is progressing in completing by Q3 more agreements with large customers, including OEMs, while SK Hynix continues to focus on the major hyperscale customers for both DDR5 and NAND flash.
We forecast HBM bit demand of 33.1bn Gb (+90% YoY) in 2026E and 58.7bn Gb (+77% YoY) in 2027E. On the supply side, we forecast (1) SK Hynix to remain the market leader in 2026E with 48% bit share (Samsung 30%, Micron 21%) and (2) Samsung to surpass SK Hynix with 41% bit share (SK Hynix 39%, Micron 20%)
• Our top pick in Memory is Samsung Electronics (APAC Key Call Buy, PT Won550k). We also have a Buy rating on SK Hynix (PT Won3.20m)

# d'Alba Global (Buy): Underappreciated B2B growth potential in US and Europe; our top séctor pick
Dalba has focused on premium positioning, pricing its products 20-30% above those of peers, while pursuing a diversification strategy across regions and channels rather than concentrating on a single market. As a result, its marketing efficiency has been relatively diluted, and with higher SKU diversification, growth has lagged APR's. That has led its valuation to trade at a discount of over 50% to APR. However, we believe this dynamic is set to shift. Starting this year, Dalba is likely to accelerate marketing investment in the US/EU as per mgmt while expanding its product portfolio, which could drive growth re-acceleration and help narrow its valuation discount.
